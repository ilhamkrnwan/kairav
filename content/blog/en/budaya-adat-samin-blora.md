# Samin Indigenous Culture in Blora: The Dawn of Honesty and Non-Violent Resistance of Sedulur Sikep

Amidst the rush of global modernization and shifting social paradigms, an indigenous community in the heartland of Blora Regency firmly preserves simplicity, absolute honesty, and environmental harmony without compromise. This community is widely known as the **Samin Indigenous People**, or more respectfully addressed by its members as **Sedulur Sikep** (*Wong Sikep*).

Exploring the Samin culture in Blora—particularly at the Karangpace hamlet in Klopoduwur Village—is a profoundly moving spiritual and intellectual journey. It invites us to question modern definitions of material progress, contemplate the essence of human integrity, and learn from a philosophy of life that served as one of the most extraordinary weapons of passive resistance against Dutch colonial rule in Indonesia.

---

## Origins: Samin Surosentiko and Civil Disobedience (1889)

The Samin movement did not emerge in a vacuum. It was founded by a charismatic visionary named **Samin Surosentiko** (born Raden Kohar in 1859 in Ploso Village, Kediren, Blora). In the late 19th century, around 1889, the Dutch colonial government tightened its monopoly over the vast teak forests of Blora through the *Boschwezen* (Colonial Forestry Service). Indigenous villagers who had depended on forest resources for generations were suddenly forbidden from harvesting timber, subjected to heavy taxation, and forced into mandatory labor.

Faced with systematic oppression, Samin Surosentiko chose neither violent insurrection nor armed conflict. Instead, he formulated a pioneering strategy of struggle based on moral and spiritual consciousness known today as **passive resistance** or *civil disobedience*—decades before Mahatma Gandhi popularized *Satyagraha* in India.

The core principles of Samin resistance were elegantly simple yet utterly dismantled colonial authority:
1. **Refusal to Pay Taxes:** Grounded in the belief that land, water, and forests belong to the Creator (*Gusti*), not to Dutch colonial administrators.
2. **Refusal of Forced Labor:** Members worked solely to sustain their families and local community needs.
3. **Use of Egaliter Javanese (*Ngoko*):** The Samin rejected speaking in aristocratic Javanese (*Krama Inggil*) to colonial officials or feudal elites, utilizing low-level Javanese (*Ngoko*) to assert that all human beings stand equal before God.
4. **Literal and Innocent Non-Cooperation:** When interrogated by colonial tax collectors, Samin followers responded with literal, humorous innocence that exasperated colonial bureaucrats.

As the movement expanded across Blora, Rembang, Pati, and Bojonegoro, Samin Surosentiko was arrested by Dutch authorities in 1907 and exiled to Padang, West Sumatra, where he passed away in 1914. However, the seeds of his philosophy remained deeply rooted in the heart of Blora.

## The Philosophy of Wong Sikep: Moral Integrity and Daily Guidelines

Members of the community decline the label "Samin" when used derogatorily by outsiders. They prefer the title **Wong Sikep** or **Sedulur Sikep**, which translates literally to "kind-hearted brethren who embrace honesty."

In the worldview of Sedulur Sikep, truth is measured not by lofty rhetoric or academic credentials, but by purity of action and sincerity of heart. They adhere strictly to **Five Ethical Prohibitions** in daily life:

1. **Ojo Drengki:** Do not envy or harbor malice toward the success or possessions of others.
2. **Ojo Srei:** Do not be greedy, malevolent, or take what does not belong to you.
3. **Ojo Panasten:** Do not be easily offended, quick to anger, or spread hatred.
4. **Ojo Dawen:** Do not accuse, slander, or judge others without verified proof.
5. **Ojo Kemeren:** Do not covet the belongings of others or foster jealousy.

For Sedulur Sikep, words are sacred vows. Once spoken, speech must align perfectly with conduct (*anindita*). Deceit, theft, adultery, and verbal abuse are strictly forbidden. Their lives are characterized by modest black attire, agriculture as their primary vocation, and courteous speech.

## Harmony with Nature: Earth as "Biyung" (Mother)

One of the most inspiring pillars of Samin culture is their profound reverence for the natural world. For Sedulur Sikep, **The Earth is *Biyung*** (Biological Mother) who provides food, water, and shelter for all living beings, while **The Sky is *Bapa*** (Father) who protects.

This belief generates a highly sophisticated environmental ethic:
- **Sustainable Organic Agriculture:** They practice subsistence farming to feed their families without resort to industrial chemicals that degrade soil vitality.
- **Teak Forest Conservation:** Living alongside Blora's legendary teak forests, they harvest only fallen wood or timber strictly necessary for home construction (*soko guru*). Deforestation and commercial exploitation are forbidden.
- **Protection of Water Sources:** Natural springs are treated as sacred lifelines that must remain unpolluted.

In an era of global climate crisis, the Sedulur Sikep worldview offers invaluable lessons on ecological stewardship and planetary sustainability.

## Klopoduwur Samin Village in the Contemporary Era

Visiting the Klopoduwur Samin Village in Banjarejo District, Blora, guests are greeted by sturdy teak wooden pavilions (*Pendopo Karangpace*). Local elders, such as Mbah Lasiyo, warmly welcome travelers and researchers seeking to understand Samin philosophy.

While contemporary Samin generations increasingly engage with modern education, technology, and civic life, the core moral compass of their ancestors remains intact. Children are taught honesty, filial piety, and reverence for nature from early childhood.

Both the Blora Regency Government and the Ministry of Education and Culture have designated Samin culture as an essential Intangible Cultural Heritage of Indonesia.

## A Call for Integrity in a Modern World

Reflecting on Samin culture in Blora reminds us that true wealth lies not in material accumulation, but in living honestly and acting with compassion toward fellow human beings and the earth.

The Sedulur Sikep of Blora demonstrate that honesty is not merely a moral ideal, but a viable way of life capable of enduring through centuries, overcoming colonial oppression, and offering profound tranquility amidst a chaotic world. From the shaded teak groves of Blora, the light of Samin integrity shines brightly for all who seek truth and meaningful living.
