# History and Magical Aura of Barongan from Blora Teak Forest: From Gembong Amijoyo Legend to Resistance Symbol

The rhythmic footsteps of dancers shake the earth as gamelan gongs, drums, and piercing traditional trumpets echo through the mystical silence of Blora’s Teak Forest. Emerging from clouds of incense smoke comes a towering tiger mask adorned with gleaming glass marble eyes and a dense black crown of palm fiber. This is **Singo Barong**, the central figure of **Blora Barongan Performance Art**—an intangible cultural heritage that serves as the beating pulse of pride for the people of Blora Regency, Central Java.

For the residents of Blora, Barongan is far more than a simple folk dance. It encapsulates ancient Javanese mythology, the history of anti-colonial resistance, and the authentic spirit of the Blora community—spontaneous, courageous in defending truth, united, and deeply familial.

---

## Panji Mythology: The Battle in Wengker Forest

The historical and cosmological roots of Blora Barongan stem from the epic **Hikayat Panji** legend. The story begins when King Klana Sawandana of the Bantarangin Kingdom fell in love with Princess Sekartaji (Dewi Galuh Candra Kirana) of the Kediri Kingdom. To deliver his proposal, King Klana dispatched his powerful grand vizier, **Patih Bujangganong** (*Pujonggo Anom*), accompanied by horse-riding warriors (*Jathilan*).

While traveling through a dense wilderness—identified in Blora tradition as the Wengker Teak Forest—the royal procession was ambushed by the mythical guardian of the forest: **Singo Barong**.

### The Legend of Gembong Amijoyo
In Blora folklore, Singo Barong represents **Adipati Gembong Amijoyo**, a powerful warrior guardian of the teak forest borders. Gembong Amijoyo is depicted as a giant tiger carrying a peacock perched atop his head, displaying its iridescent feathers.

An intense battle ensued between Bujangganong and Singo Barong. The conflict escalated with the arrival of **Joko Lodra** (Gembong Amijoyo's martial arts brother aligned with the Jenggala Kingdom) and cavalry troops. This dramatic clash of mystical powers forms the narrative core visualized in the Blora Barongan dance today.

## Craftsmanship Adaptation: Replacing Peacock Feathers with Teak Forest Fiber

A key feature distinguishing **Blora Barongan** from Reog Ponorogo lies in the materials used for the mask and crown (*dadak merak*).

During the 19th century, when Blora artists sought to preserve this art form in rural forest villages, acquiring large quantities of genuine peacock feathers was extremely difficult. Demonstrating resourcefulness, local artisans substituted peacock feathers with **black sugar palm fiber (*ijuk*) and teak forest bark**, woven densely into a menacing black crown, with peacock feathers placed selectively at the crest.

This modification created the iconic visual identity of Blora Barongan:
1. **Glass Marble Eyes:** The Singo Barong mask features large glass marbles (*kelendhi*) for eyes. Reflecting night lights, they symbolize sharpness of vision, vigilance, and unyielding courage.
2. **Mystical and Commanding Presence:** The black fiber crown swaying dramatically during dance movements evokes an intense, supernatural aura as if the forest tiger has awakened.
3. **Clacking Wooden Jaw (*Caplokan*):** The rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* sound produced by clapping the wooden jaws of the mask creates a captivating auditory beat unique to Blora.

## Barongan as Samin Surosentiko's Anti-Colonial Resistance Media

Fewer people realize that Blora Barongan shares a profound historical connection with the resistance movement of **Samin Surosentiko** during Dutch colonial rule (late 19th to early 20th century).

After exploring cultural traditions in Sumoroto, Samin Surosentiko recognized the potential of Barongan as a tool for community organizing. At the time, the Dutch colonial government strictly banned political gatherings and assembly. To bypass these restrictions, Samin and his followers utilized Barongan performances as a *camouflaged gathering*.

When drums and gongs resounded across village squares, hundreds of villagers from surrounding teak forests gathered to watch. Interspersed between mesmerizing dance acts, Samin and community elders shared moral teachings, encouraged self-reliance, advocated non-payment of colonial taxes, and nurtured non-violent resistance against colonial tyranny. Barongan transformed from folk entertainment into a symbol of cultural resilience and political empowerment.

## Social Functions, Lamporan Rituals, and National Recognition

To this day, Barongan plays a vital role in Blora's social and spiritual life. Beyond cultural celebrations, weddings, and municipal anniversaries, Barongan is integrated into sacred rituals such as **Lamporan** (an exorcism ritual to ward off epidemics and crop pests) and **Sedekah Bumi** (village cleansing ceremonies).

During the *Lamporan* ritual, Barongan troupes navigate village borders at night carrying flaming torches to purify the area of negative energy. Locals believe the commanding presence of Singo Barong repels evil spirits and ensures community safety.

In recognition of its historical, artistic, and philosophical significance, the Government of Indonesia officially designated **Blora Barongan Art** as an **Intangible Cultural Heritage (WBTB)** in 2009. This designation solidified Blora's title as the **City of Barongan**.

---

## Preserving the Cultural Flame in the Modern Era

Today, Blora Barongan continues to evolve while maintaining its authentic core. Youth troupes collaborate traditional gamelan with trumpets, drums, and modern instruments to engage younger generations.

Seeing young children in Blora villages imitating Bujangganong dance moves with handmade masks is living proof that this ancestral heritage remains vibrant. Blora Barongan is a story of bravery, integrity, and love for the land—a masterpiece born from the depths of the teak forest to illuminate Indonesian culture.
