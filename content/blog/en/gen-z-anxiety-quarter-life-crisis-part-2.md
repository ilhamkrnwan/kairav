# Crisis in the Twenties: When Anxiety, Burnout, and Quarter-Life Crisis Hit Gen Z (Part 2)

Following our analysis of macroeconomic pressures, corporate layoffs, and financial strains in [Part One](/blogs/gen-z-badai-ekonomi-phk-kebijakan-part-1), a fundamental question emerges: **what is the hidden psychological cost of these external forces on the youth?**

For young adults in their twenties, the transition into adulthood should ideally represent a period of personal discovery, identity formation, and foundational growth. For Gen Z, however, this phase has evolved into an era of heightened psychological vulnerability. Concepts such as *mental health*, *anxiety*, *burnout*, and *quarter-life crisis* are no longer mere social media trends; they reflect daily struggles for millions.

This article forms **Part Two** of our Gen Z series, specifically dissecting the psychological landscape and inner turmoil facing young adults today.

---

## 1. Chronic Anxiety and Fear of the Future

One of the most pervasive consequences of economic uncertainty is the prevalence of **generalized anxiety** and existential dread.

Why is Gen Z uniquely vulnerable to chronic anxiety?
- **Fear of Failure:** In an intensely competitive labor market, there is a pervasive perception that a single setback—such as a job loss or delayed career start—can permanently derail future prospects.
- **Information Overload and *Doomscrolling*:** Living 24/7 in a digital ecosystem exposes young adults to a continuous newsfeed of global crises: layoff reports, rising living costs, environmental degradation, and geopolitical conflicts. Human neurology is not wired to process continuous macro-tragedies without psychological fatigue.
- **Financial Insecurity:** Persistent worries regarding monthly rent payments, emergency savings adequacy, or healthcare coverage without comprehensive insurance.

---

## 2. *Quarter-Life Crisis*: Directional Confusion Amid Social Pressure

Entering the 20 to 25 age bracket, most Gen Z individuals encounter a **Quarter-Life Crisis**—a profound phase of self-doubt regarding life direction, career trajectory, relationships, and personal identity.

This crisis is intensified by the **Social Comparison Trap** inherent in digital media platforms. Browsing LinkedIn or Instagram presents a curated stream of peer accomplishments: 23-year-old corporate managers, young homeowners, or high-earning entrepreneurs.

While intellectually aware that social media displays curated highlight reels, the subconscious mind frequently interprets these images as evidence of personal inadequacy. This fosters *imposter syndrome*, troubling reflections such as *"Why am I falling behind?"*, and a feeling of failure before life has truly unfolded.

---

## 3. *Burnout* and Chronic Mental Exhaustion

Older generations sometimes mischaracterize Gen Z as overly fragile or lacking resilience. Empirical evidence, however, demonstrates the opposite: **Gen Z is among the hardest-working cohorts, encountering severe burnout and mental exhaustion at remarkably early stages of adulthood.**

To navigate economic demands, many young workers adopt intense *hustle culture*—balancing full-time roles with late-night freelance commitments, sacrificing sleep and social well-being.

Sustained overwork eventually strains the nervous system, leaving the body in chronic stress (*flight-or-fight mode*), marked by:
- Severe sleep disturbances and insomnia.
- Loss of motivation and emotional numbness.
- Persistent fatigue unmitigated by short rest periods.
- Psychosomatic symptoms including digestive distress and muscle tension.

---

## Acknowledging the Struggle: "IT IS OKAY TO NOT BE OKAY"

The initial step toward recovery requires honest acknowledgment without shame. The anxiety, exhaustion, and confusion experienced by Gen Z are not indicators of personal weakness; they represent understandable human responses to extraordinary external demands.

Mental well-being cannot be restored by masking struggles behind toxic positivity or pretending external pressures do not exist.
